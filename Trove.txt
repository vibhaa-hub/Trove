## 1. Executive summary

When someone dies, their family has to find out what they owned, prove they are entitled to it, and claim it from each institution one at a time. Most families start with a death certificate, a phone number and an email address. Assets sit in banks, insurers, pension funds, brokers, registries and crypto exchanges that do not talk to each other. Money that nobody finds ends up in dormant or unclaimed pools. Money that is found is often claimed slowly, and sometimes fraudulently.

Trove is an AI-assisted inheritance platform with two modes on one data model:

- **Plan Mode (before death).** A person records their assets, documents, nominees and instructions in an encrypted vault. On death, nothing is released until a sequence of identity, legal and fraud gates is passed.
- **Discovery Mode (after death).** The person never used Trove. The family uploads whatever they have (SMS backups, email exports, a single bank statement, cloud folders, documents) and Trove turns those traces into ranked asset leads, a coverage map of what is still missing, and claim packs for each institution.

Both modes feed one knowledge graph, one trust engine, one claims workspace and one audit trail.

**Why now.** A very large generational wealth transfer is under way. Regulators in India, the UK and the US are pushing institutions to reunite unclaimed assets with owners. Families keep financial lives in email, SMS and apps rather than paper. Language and encoder models can now read those traces cheaply and reliably.

**Who pays.** Families pay for planning and for discovery cases. Banks, insurers, wealth managers and employers pay to offer Trove to their customers and staff (B2B2C), because it cuts their unclaimed liabilities, claim-handling costs and fraud, and helps them keep the next generation as clients.

**Where it stands.** A working prototype exists: Plan Mode with a 10-gate release process, Discovery Mode that reads SMS, email, cloud exports and statements into leads, a knowledge graph with fraud insights, country rules for India, UK, US and EU, an encrypted vault, and an AI case assistant that runs on local encoder models with an optional LLM.

**Recommended launch.** India first (largest unclaimed pool, strong digital public infrastructure, SMS-heavy financial footprint), then the UK. See assumption A1.

## 2. Problem statement

### 2.1 The core problem

> Families cannot see, prove or claim what a person owned when that person dies, and nothing connects the institutions that hold it.

### 2.2 Why it happens

| # | Root cause | What the family experiences |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Assets are spread across many institutions, each with its own records | No single place lists what the person owned |
| P2 | Beneficiaries have no visibility while the owner is alive | Heirs do not know which banks, policies or funds exist |
| P3 | Records are fragmented across paper, email, SMS, apps and devices | Evidence exists but is scattered and locked |
| P4 | Nominations are missing, outdated or wrong | Claims stall or go to the wrong person |
| P5 | Wills are missing, unregistered or cannot be found | Intestate succession, disputes and court delays |
| P6 | Digital assets (crypto, online accounts, wallets) are hidden or locked | Value is lost permanently when keys are lost |
| P7 | Pensions and employer benefits are forgotten after job changes | Pots go untraced for years |
| P8 | Each institution has its own claim process, forms and evidence rules | Families repeat the same paperwork many times |
| P9 | Death is an easy moment for fraud | Fake death claims, impersonation, forged documents, rogue relatives |
| P10 | Families do this while grieving, often with no legal or financial background | Mistakes, abandonment of claims, reliance on costly agents |

### 2.3 The result

```
Unknown assets
  + missing documents
  + manual investigation
  + fraud risk
  + one claim process per institution
  = delayed, incomplete or lost wealth transfer
```

### 2.4 Scale of the problem (indicative)

| Market | Indicator | Source to verify |
|---|---|---|
| India | Unclaimed bank deposits transferred to the RBI's Depositor Education and Awareness (DEA) Fund ran to roughly ₹78,000 crore by March 2024 | RBI Annual Report |
| India | Unclaimed shares and dividends held by IEPF; unclaimed insurance amounts held by insurers; dormant EPF accounts | IEPF Authority, IRDAI, EPFO disclosures |
| UK | Lost pension pots estimated at around £26bn across ~2.8m pots (2022) | Pensions Policy Institute |
| US | About 1 in 7 people have unclaimed property; states hold tens of billions of dollars | NAUPA / MissingMoney.com |
| Global | Tens of trillions of dollars expected to pass between generations over the next two decades | Cerulli Associates and wealth reports |

### 2.5 Problem statement for the pitch

> Every year, billions in deposits, insurance, pensions and investments go unclaimed or are claimed fraudulently because families cannot find what a person owned or prove their right to it. Trove gives families a single, verified way to plan, discover and claim an inheritance, and gives institutions a safe, low-cost way to release it to the right people.

|---|---|---|---|
| **Asset owner** (35–75, salaried or retired, has 5–30 financial relationships) | Alive, wants family protected | "Make sure my family can find and claim everything without a fight" | Writing a list is tedious; sharing passwords is unsafe; a will alone does not list accounts |

| **Lawyer / estate practitioner** | Handles succession and probate | "Get a complete, evidenced estate file quickly" | Clients arrive with shoeboxes of paper |

## 4. Solution overview

Trove is one platform with two entry points that end in the same place.

```
                         TROVE
                           |
          +----------------+----------------+
          v                                 v
     PLAN MODE                        DISCOVERY MODE
     owner is alive                   owner has died, never used Trove
     - register assets                - say what you have
     - vault documents                - upload SMS / email / cloud / statement
     - add nominees                   - connect mailbox / folder (lawful access)
     - set release policy             - query official tracing services
          |                                 |
          +---------------+-----------------+
                          v
              COMMON DATA PLATFORM + KNOWLEDGE GRAPH
                          v
            TRUST, IDENTITY AND RELEASE ENGINE
       (death verification, heir verification, fraud scoring)
                          v
                  CLAIMS WORKSPACE
       (per-institution claim packs, tasks, deadlines)
                          v
               TRANSFER AND SETTLEMENT
          (tracked per asset, full audit trail)
```

**Design principles**

1. **Start from whatever the family has.** One SMS backup or one bank statement is enough to begin.
2. **Evidence before conclusions.** Every lead links back to the exact source line that produced it. Nothing is a fact until a human or an institution confirms it.
3. **Humans approve, AI assists.** AI finds, sorts and drafts. People approve deaths, heirs, matches and releases.
4. **Lawful access only.** Trove never guesses passwords, bypasses device locks or scrapes accounts it is not authorised to read.
5. **Minimum data.** Raw email and document text is processed in memory. Trove stores the extracted signal, a masked reference and a digest, not the original content (except vaulted documents, which are encrypted).
6. **One country profile per case.** Legal documents, deadlines, privacy rules and tracing services follow the country of the estate.

## 7. Operating model and actors

| Actor | Role in Trove | Typical permissions |
|---|---|---|
| Asset owner | Creates Plan case, vault, nominees, release policy | Full control of own case; can veto any release while alive |
| Nominee / beneficiary | Enrols via invite, verifies identity, raises or confirms a death claim, receives released documents | Only their own record and their granted document types after release |
| Executor | Administers the estate | Sees all documents after release; drives claims |
| Family member (Discovery) | Opens a Discovery case, uploads sources, reviews leads | Own case only |
| Independent reviewer | Approves ID documents, death certificates and legal documents; overrides high-risk blocks with notes | Only items in their review queue; mandatory TOTP |
| Institution user (Planned) | Receives claim packs, confirms or rejects assets, records payment | Only claims addressed to their institution |
| Partner advisor / lawyer (Planned) | Works cases on behalf of families | Cases the family shares with them |
| Platform administrator | Operates the service | No access to case content by default; break-glass access is logged and alerted |

## 8. Plan Mode (before death)

### 8.1 What the owner does

| Step | Action | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Create account, choose country (drives legal rules) | Built |
| 2 | Register assets: bank, deposit, insurance, mutual fund, shares, pension/PF, property, business, crypto, digital accounts | Built |
| 3 | Upload documents to the encrypted vault: will, trust deed, property papers, policies, tax returns, ID | Built |
| 4 | Add nominees with the email and phone they must prove they control, optional ID last-4 and DOB (stored only as keyed hashes), and the document types each may see | Built |
| 5 | Hand each nominee a one-time invite code in person | Built |
| 6 | Set release policy: quorum (k of n nominees), cooling-off period, check-in frequency, legal-document requirement | Built |
| 7 | Periodic "I am alive" check-ins and reminders | Built |
| 8 | Import assets automatically from consented data feeds (Account Aggregator in India, Open Banking in the UK, consolidated account statements) | Planned |
| 9 | Nomination health check: flag assets with no nominee or a nominee who differs from the will | Planned |
| 10 | Record instructions and letters to family (funeral wishes, digital accounts, who to call) | Planned |
| 11 | Sealed secrets for crypto and passwords, split so no single party (including Trove) can open them alone | Planned |

### 8.2 Data sources

Manual entry, document upload (with extraction), consent-based financial data feeds (Account Aggregator, Open Banking), consolidated statements from depositories and fund registrars, and guided setup of each big-tech legacy feature (Google Inactive Account Manager, Apple Legacy Contact, Facebook memorialisation).

### 8.3 Outputs

Asset register, beneficiary register, encrypted vault, inheritance instructions, release policy, claims-readiness score per asset (has nominee, has documents, institution known, contact details current).

### 8.4 Release on death (summary; detail in Section 12)

Nothing is released until all ten gates pass: invite code, email OTP, phone OTP, authenticator MFA, ID match and human ID review, reviewer-approved death certificate, k-of-n nominee confirmations, owner alert with veto, cooling-off period, and fraud-risk scoring.

## 9. Discovery Mode (after death)

### 9.1 Starting point

The family typically has some of: death certificate, full name, date of birth, national ID number, an email address, a phone number, maybe the unlocked phone, maybe one or two documents. Often little else.

### 9.2 Objective

Find bank accounts, deposits, insurance, investments, pensions and provident funds, property, business holdings, loans and cards (liabilities matter too), crypto and digital assets.

### 9.3 Journey

| Step | What happens | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1. What do you have? | Family ticks what they hold and adds known emails, phones, employers. Trove builds a prioritised starting plan | Built |
| 2. Authority check | Death certificate uploaded and reviewed; relationship to the deceased recorded. Institution-facing steps stay locked until authority is shown | Built (review gate) |
| 3. Add sources | SMS export, email export or IMAP, cloud ZIP, folder/USB, statements, documents | Built |
| 4. Leads | Each source produces signals. Signals that point to the same institution and asset merge into a lead whose confidence rises with independent corroboration | Built |
| 5. Review | Family promotes real leads to the asset register or dismisses them | Built |
| 6. Coverage map | Shows which asset classes are still gaps and where to look next, including official tracing services | Built |
| 7. Official tracing | Guided searches of government and industry tracing services; results added as evidence | Partial (guidance Built; automated queries Planned) |
| 8. Institution confirmation | Trove sends a structured asset enquiry to participating institutions; their answer confirms or rejects the lead | Planned |
| 9. Claims | Confirmed assets become claims with packs, checklists and deadlines | Partial |
| 10. Settlement | Each claim tracked to payment or transfer; estate summary exported for the executor and lawyer | Partial |

## 10. Discovery mechanisms (detailed)

Each mechanism has an input, a method, what it finds, a legal basis, and a status. Mechanisms are ordered by how often they produce results and how easy they are to use.

### DM1. Phone SMS discovery — Built

- **Input:** SMS backup export (.xml from backup apps, .csv, .txt).
- **Method:** Sender-ID and pattern rules for banks, card issuers, insurers, brokers, AMCs, PF and pension bodies; encoder-model classification for lines no rule matched; masked last digits extracted.
- **Finds:** Savings and current accounts, cards, loans (EMIs), insurance premiums, SIPs, demat and broking activity, PF credits.
- **Why it matters:** In India almost every financial event triggers an SMS. This is often the single richest source.
- **Legal basis:** Family member with lawful possession of the device or its backup.

### DM2. Email discovery — Built (export + IMAP), Planned (OAuth)

- **Input:** .mbox (Google Takeout) or .eml upload; IMAP read-only connection; later Gmail API and Microsoft Graph.
- **Method:** Headers scanned first; only finance-looking messages downloaded (BODY.PEEK, nothing marked read); attachments sent to document extraction; app password used once and never stored.
- **Finds:** Policy renewals, FD maturity notices, broker contract notes, fund statements, pension letters, crypto exchange notices, property tax and utility bills (property clues).
- **Pipeline:**

```
Mailbox connector -> header filter -> message fetch -> attachment processor
   -> document extraction -> entity extraction -> signals -> leads -> knowledge graph
```

- **Legal basis:** Access must be lawful. In Plan Mode the owner pre-authorises. In Discovery Mode, access comes through the provider's deceased-user process, an executor's legal authority, or a family member who already has lawful access. Rules differ by country (for example RUFADAA in most US states). Counsel review required per country.

### DM3. Cloud storage discovery — Built (ZIP export), Planned (OAuth)

- **Input:** ZIP of Drive / OneDrive / Dropbox / iCloud folders, or a file listing; later direct OAuth connectors.
- **Method:** File-name signals (for example Last_Will.pdf, LIC_policy.pdf) plus text inside TXT/CSV/PDF; zip-bomb and path-traversal guards.
- **Finds:** Wills, trust deeds, policies, property papers, investment statements, tax returns.

### DM4. Device and local storage discovery — Built (folder/USB/NAS), Planned (desktop agent)

- **Input:** A read-only mounted folder, USB drive or NAS; later a signed desktop agent the family runs on the deceased's laptop.
- **Method:** Runs locally; classifies files on the device; uploads only matches the family approves.
- **Finds:** PDF statements, spreadsheets of investments, tax files, wallet files, password-manager exports (flagged, never opened without explicit instruction).

### DM5. Statement and transaction trail analysis — Built

- **Input:** One bank statement is ideal.
- **Method:** Every line is classified. Recurring debits reveal insurance premiums, SIPs, loan EMIs, card payments, rent paid. Recurring credits reveal salary, pension, dividends, interest, rent received.
- **Finds:** The "money trail" to other institutions. One statement often points to five or more further assets.

### DM6. Document upload and extraction — Built (text PDF), Planned (image OCR)

- **Input:** Statements, tax returns, letters, policy schedules, property documents, ID documents.
- **Method:** Text extraction, field extraction (institution, asset type, masked reference, dates, amounts), document type classification.
- **Finds:** Institution names, account and policy numbers (masked), property references, holdings.

### DM7. Tax record discovery — Planned (high priority for India)

- **Input:** Tax statements the heir can lawfully obtain (in India the Annual Information Statement and Form 26AS through the legal heir registration on the income-tax portal; elsewhere equivalent records).
- **Finds:** Every bank paying interest, every company paying dividends, mutual fund redemptions, property transactions, rent. This is one of the most complete single sources of financial relationships.

### DM8. Consolidated holdings statements — Planned

- **Input:** Depository consolidated account statements (NSDL/CDSL CAS), mutual fund consolidated statements (via the registrars or MF Central) in India; equivalents in other markets.
- **Finds:** All demat holdings and mutual fund folios across fund houses.

### DM9. Official tracing services — Partial

| Country | Service (verify current availability) | Finds |
|---|---|---|
| India | RBI UDGAM portal | Unclaimed deposits across participating banks |
| India | IEPF Authority | Unclaimed shares and dividends |
| India | EPFO (UAN-based) | Provident fund and pension |
| India | Insurer unclaimed-amount search on each insurer's website | Unclaimed policy money |
| UK | Pension Tracing Service; MyLostAccount; HMRC; Gretel-style services | Pensions, dormant bank and building society accounts, NS&I |
| UK | Tell Us Once; Death Notification Service | Notifies government and participating banks |
| US | MissingMoney.com / state unclaimed property offices; NAIC Life Insurance Policy Locator | Unclaimed property; life policies |

### DM10. Banking discovery network — Planned (B2B, needs partners)

- **Method:** With verified death and verified heir authority, Trove sends a single structured asset enquiry (name, DOB, ID, phone, email) to participating banks. Each bank answers "relationship exists / does not exist" and its claims route. No balances are shared until a claim is lodged.
- **Finds:** Accounts, deposits, lockers, loans.
- **Why banks join:** Fewer branch visits, cleaner claim files, lower dormant balances, regulatory goodwill.

### DM11. Insurance discovery — Planned (network) / Partial (signals)

Life, health, group/employer cover, annuities, credit-life attached to loans. Signals from SMS, email, statements; confirmation through insurer participation or industry policy-locator services.

### DM12. Pension and provident fund discovery — Partial

Employer history prompts the family to list past employers; each maps to the PF or pension body and the tracing route for that country.

### DM13. Property discovery — Planned

Sources: property tax receipts and utility bills in email, registered sale deeds in the vault, land and property registries where the country allows searches by name, mortgage registries, municipal records.

### DM14. Investment discovery — Partial

Contract notes, dividend credits, fund statements, broker SMS, CAS statements, tax records.

### DM15. Crypto and digital asset discovery — Partial

- Exchange sign-up and transaction emails, KYC notices, app lists on the phone, wallet addresses found in files.
- Public blockchain lookups on found addresses (read-only).
- Exchange claims through each exchange's deceased-user process.
- Trove never attempts to brute-force keys or seed phrases.

### DM16. Credit report discovery — Planned

A credit bureau report (where heirs can obtain it) lists loans and cards, which point to banks and sometimes to credit-life insurance.

### DM17. Human network discovery — Planned

Prompts the family to list the deceased's CA/accountant, lawyer, advisor, employer HR and bank relationship manager, and generates letters asking each what they know.

## 11. Discovery engine: leads, confidence and coverage

### 11.1 Lifecycle

```
Signal  ->  Lead  ->  Candidate asset  ->  Confirmed asset  ->  Claim  ->  Settled
(raw      (merged     (promoted by        (confirmed by       (lodged    (paid /
mention)   by inst.    family)             institution or      with       transferred)
           + type)                         strong documents)   inst.)
                         \-> Dismissed (kept for audit, never re-suggested)
```

### 11.2 Confidence score (baseline formula)

```
lead_confidence = 1 - PRODUCT over sources s of (1 - w_source(s) * m(s) * r(s))

w_source  source reliability   (institution answer 0.95, statement 0.8, email 0.7, SMS 0.6, file name 0.4)
m         match strength       (exact masked reference 1.0, institution + type 0.7, institution only 0.4)
r         recency              (1.0 if within 24 months, decaying to 0.5 at 10 years)
```

Independent sources (different channels) compound. Two signals from the same channel count once with a small bonus. Bands: **High ≥ 0.8**, **Medium 0.5–0.8**, **Low < 0.5**. The current prototype uses a simpler corroboration count; this formula is the baseline target.

### 11.3 Coverage map

For each asset class the case shows: found / searched with nothing found / not yet searched, and the next best action (for example "No pension found. Add employer history or search EPFO"). This is how the family knows when they are done.

## 12. Trust, identity and release engine

### 12.1 Plan Mode release gates — Built

| # | Gate | Stops |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | One-time invite code (7-day expiry) + registered email + strong password | Strangers enrolling |
| 2 | Email OTP (hashed, 10 min, 5 tries) | Wrong person using the email |
| 3 | Phone OTP | Single-channel compromise |
| 4 | Authenticator TOTP MFA, replay-protected | Password theft |
| 5 | ID last-4 + DOB matched to owner-registered hashes; ID document reviewed by a human; 3 mismatches suspend and alert | Impersonation |
| 6 | Death certificate uploaded by a verified nominee and approved by an independent reviewer; name cross-checked | Fake death claims |
| 7 | k-of-n nominee confirmations, each with fresh TOTP; any dispute freezes release | One rogue relative |
| 8 | Owner alerted by email and phone when a claim opens; "I am alive" cancels it | Claims against a living owner |
| 9 | Cooling-off period (default 72 h; country minimums apply) | Rushed fraud |
| 10 | Fraud-risk score; HIGH blocks release unless a reviewer records an override note | Pattern-based fraud |

After release, each nominee sees only granted document types; downloads need TOTP step-up within 10 minutes; every action enters a per-case hash-chained audit log.

### 12.2 Death verification levels

| Level | Evidence | Unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| D0 | Family states a death | Discovery from family-held sources only |
| D1 | Death certificate uploaded and reviewed by a human | Official tracing guidance, claim drafting |
| D2 | Certificate verified against a government registry or issuing authority (Planned) | Institution enquiries on the network |
| D3 | Court or government entitlement document (succession certificate, probate, letters of administration, legal-heir certificate) approved | Claim submission and release of high-value assets |

### 12.3 Heir and authority verification

Identity proofing of the claimant (ID document + liveness through a KYC provider, Planned), relationship evidence (marriage or birth certificate, family register), and the country's entitlement document. Trove records evidence and reviewer decisions. It does not decide legal heirship; it presents evidence to the institution that does.

## 14. AI architecture and agents

### 14.1 Principle: encoders first, generative model only when needed

Most of Trove's AI work is classification, matching and search, not writing. These run on a small local encoder model (default bge-small-en-v1.5 via ONNX, about 67 MB, CPU, no per-call cost). A generative LLM is used only for explanation, drafting and open questions, and only after explicit user consent. This keeps cost low, keeps raw data local and reduces hallucination risk.

```
Sources: SMS | email | cloud | files | statements | tax | institution answers
                         v
     Parsing + text extraction (PDF text now; OCR Planned)
                         v
     Rules (sender IDs, patterns)  +  encoder classifier (declines when unsure)
                         v
     Field extraction (institution, asset type, masked ref, date, amount)
                         v
     Entity resolution (people, institutions, accounts)
                         v
     Knowledge graph  ->  discovery engine (leads, confidence, coverage)
                         v
     Case assistant (skills + RAG; LLM optional, with consent)
                         v
     Recommendations to humans: promote lead, request document, file claim
```

### 14.2 Agents as skills, not separate bots

The assistant is one runtime that loads **skills** on demand (progressive disclosure: catalogue of one-line descriptions, then the skill's instructions, then reference files, then read-only case tools). Tool output never includes names, contacts, IDs or account numbers.

| Skill / agent | Job | Status |
|---|---|---|

### 14.3 AI safety rules

1. Document and email text is untrusted input; it can never issue instructions to the assistant (prompt-injection defence).
2. LLM outputs are restricted to known asset types and schemas; free text is never written into records without review.
3. Every extracted fact stores a pointer to its source (document ID, line or message digest).
4. AI never approves a death, an heir, a match above the auto-link threshold without audit, or a release.
5. Evaluation sets per country and source type; accuracy tracked as KPIs (Section 6); regression tests on every model change.
6. LLM provider is swappable behind one gateway (Mistral today; any provider with an in-region, no-training data agreement).

### 17.4 Sequence: Discovery from an SMS export

```
Family -> Web: upload sms.xml
Web -> API: POST /cases/{id}/discovery/sources
API -> Discovery: parse in memory (defusedxml), size and type checks
Discovery -> Rules/Encoder: classify each message (decline if unsure)
Discovery -> DB: insert signals (institution, type, masked ref, digest only)
Discovery -> Lead engine: merge signals into leads, recompute confidence
Lead engine -> Graph: upsert nodes/edges, run insights
API -> Web: leads + coverage map
Family -> Web: promote lead -> asset (CANDIDATE) -> claim later
Audit: source.uploaded, signal.extracted x N, lead.created x M
```

### 17.5 Sequence: Plan Mode release

```
Nominee A -> enrol (invite + email + password) -> email OTP -> phone OTP -> TOTP -> ID check
Nominee A -> upload death certificate -> Reviewer approves
Nominee A -> open release request
System -> Owner: email + SMS alert ("I am alive" link)
Nominees B..k -> confirm with fresh TOTP (any dispute -> freeze)
Worker -> wait for cooling-off; compute fraud score
  HIGH -> blocked until reviewer override note
  else -> release: each nominee sees only granted document types
Audit chain records each step
```

### 17.6 Entity resolution algorithm

1. **Normalise:** case-fold, strip honorifics and legal suffixes (Ltd, Pvt, Bank of), transliterate Indic names to a common Latin form, normalise phones to E.164.
2. **Block:** group candidates by institution, phone, email or name sound-alike key.
3. **Score:** deterministic keys (same national-ID hash, same full account reference) = 1.0; otherwise a weighted score from name similarity (Jaro-Winkler + encoder embedding cosine), DOB, phone, email, address.
4. **Decide:** ≥ 0.95 auto-link with audit; 0.75–0.95 human review queue; < 0.75 kept separate.
5. **Never merge automatically** across different national-ID hashes.

### 17.7 Vault encryption (target)

- Envelope encryption: one data key per document (AES-256-GCM); data keys wrapped by a per-case key; per-case keys wrapped by a regional key in KMS/HSM with rotation.
- Crypto-shredding: deleting the per-case key makes all its documents unreadable, including in backups.
- Sealed secrets (Planned): the owner's secret (for example a seed phrase) is encrypted on the owner's device and split with Shamir secret sharing (for example 2 of 3: nominee, executor, Trove). Trove releases its share only after the release gates pass, and cannot open the secret alone.
- Today: Fernet encryption for vault files, contact fields, TOTP secrets and queued messages, with the key held as a deployment secret.

### 17.8 Fraud score (baseline rules, Built)

Recent owner check-in; check-in after claimed death; future death date; certificate name mismatch; nominee added < 30 days before claim; identity mismatches; repeated auth failures; disputes; shared contacts between nominees (graph). Score bands LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH. A learned model is added once labelled outcomes exist (Phase 3).

### 22.1 Frameworks by market (to be assessed with counsel)

| Market | Privacy | Financial / sector | Succession documents (release gate) |

| Global | ISO 27001, ISO 27701, SOC 2 Type II (target certifications) | | |

The prototype already ships country profiles (succession process, required documents, deadlines from date of death, privacy rights, data residency, tracing services). None of this is a claim of compliance or certification.

### 22.2 Important legal realities that shape the design

1. **Consent-based data feeds stop at death.** Account Aggregator (India) and Open Banking (UK/EU) need the account holder's live consent. They are useful in Plan Mode only. Discovery Mode must use legal-heir authority and institution claims processes.
2. **Regulated-entity requirement.** To consume Account Aggregator data Trove must be, or partner with, a regulated Financial Information User. UK Open Banking access needs FCA authorisation as an AISP or an agent arrangement.
3. **Accessing a deceased person's accounts.** Email, cloud and phone providers have their own deceased-user processes and terms. Trove only uses access that is lawful for the person using it, and guides families to the official routes.
4. **Trove does not decide heirship.** It organises evidence; courts and institutions decide.
5. **Fees on recovered money.** Success fees on unclaimed assets are regulated or capped in some places, and "asset recovery agents" have a poor reputation. Trove caps its fees and publishes them (Section 26).
6. **Legal services boundary.** Trove must not give legal advice where that requires a licence. It partners with law firms for advice and court filings.

### 22.3 Privacy by design

Lawful basis recorded per processing purpose; consent records with withdrawal; DPIA before launch; retention schedule (for example case data deleted or archived 24 months after settlement unless the family keeps the vault); data subject rights (export and delete already exist); processor agreements with every vendor; breach response within regulatory deadlines.

- Revenue: paid pilots with institutions from the pre-seed stage.

Market size (Section 23), why now (Section 1), defensibility (Section 25), regulatory risk (Sections 22 and 30), trust and safety (Sections 12, 20, 21), unit economics (Section 26), team and roadmap (Sections 28–29).

## 29. Roadmap and MVP scope

### 29.1 Current state (prototype) — Built

Plan Mode with 10 gates, nominee portal, reviewer console, encrypted vault, SMS/email/cloud/folder/statement discovery, leads and coverage map, knowledge graph with insights, claims tracker, country profiles (IN/UK/US/EU), skills-based assistant with local encoders and optional LLM, Docker production stack, accessibility tests, end-to-end tests.

### 29.2 Phases

| Phase | Timeline (from baseline) | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| **Phase 1: MVP launch (India)** | Months 0–6 | OCR for scanned documents and photos; Gmail and Microsoft OAuth connectors; India tracing playbooks (UDGAM, IEPF, EPFO, insurers, CAS, AIS); claim pack generator for top 20 Indian banks and insurers; KYC provider; KMS-managed keys; Hindi UI; pen test; DPDP notices and consent flows; payments |
| **Phase 2: Institutions** | Months 6–12 | Partner portal; asset enquiry API; first bank and insurer pilots; Account Aggregator import for Plan Mode (via regulated partner); React Native app; advisor console; nomination health check |
| **Phase 3: Network and scale** | Months 12–24 | Multi-institution network; cross-case fraud analytics; learned fraud model; sealed secrets; ISO 27001 certification; SOC 2 Type I |
| **Phase 4: UK** | Months 18–30 | UK profile hardening, pension tracing integrations, probate partners, FCA perimeter work |

### 29.3 MVP acceptance criteria

1. A family with only an SMS export and one statement reaches at least 3 correct leads in under 15 minutes (measured on a test set of 50 consented or synthetic estates).
2. Lead precision ≥ 85% on that test set.
3. Every Plan Mode release passes all ten gates; end-to-end tests cover veto, dispute, high-risk block and lockout.
4. External penetration test with no open critical or high findings.
5. DPIA completed; privacy notice, consent and deletion flows live.
6. Claim packs generated for at least 20 institutions.

## 30. Risks, assumptions, open questions, change log

### 30.1 Key risks

| # | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Institutions slow to join the network | High | High | Lead with family-side discovery that works without them; paid pilots; sandbox and regulator engagement |
| R2 | Legal limits on accessing a deceased person's email/cloud | Medium | High | Lawful-access-only design; official provider routes; counsel opinions per country |
| R3 | A fraudulent release | Low | Very high | Ten gates, reviewer separation of duties, insurance, incident playbook |
| R4 | Data breach | Low | Very high | Minimum data, encryption, regional isolation, certifications |
| R5 | Being seen as a "recovery agent" charging on families' grief | Medium | High | Capped, published fees; free basics; clear value |
| R6 | Low willingness to pay before death (Plan Mode) | High | Medium | Distribute through banks, employers, advisors; free tier |
| R7 | AI extraction errors | Medium | Medium | Human review, evidence links, evaluation sets, decline-when-unsure |
| R8 | Regulatory classification (is Trove a regulated activity?) | Medium | High | Early legal opinions; partner with regulated entities where needed |
| R9 | Big tech or a large bank builds a similar feature | Medium | Medium | Cross-institution neutrality is the point; a single bank cannot see other banks |

### 30.2 Assumptions

| # | Assumption | Owner to validate |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Launch in India first, UK second | Founders |
| A2 | Families will upload SMS and email exports if the privacy model is clear | Product (user research) |
| A3 | At least one bank or insurer will pay for a pilot within 12 months | Business development |
| A4 | Self-serve Discovery pricing in Section 26 is acceptable | Product (pricing tests) |
| A5 | Local encoder models are accurate enough for bulk classification in Indian financial text | AI lead |

### 30.3 Open questions (details that still need a decision)

1. Legal entity, founding team, advisors (legal, banking, insurance) and board.
2. Final brand and domain; trademark check for "Trove" in each launch country.
3. Exact first customer: families directly, or one institution as design partner?
4. Which regulated partner for Account Aggregator access in India?
5. KYC, e-sign and OCR vendors per region.
6. Liability and insurance: professional indemnity and cyber cover; terms that limit liability for institution decisions.
7. Disputes between heirs: does Trove freeze, mediate, or refer to lawyers? (Prototype: any dispute freezes release.)
8. Minors and persons lacking capacity as beneficiaries: guardian flows.
9. Cross-border estates (assets in two countries): one case with two profiles, or linked cases?
10. Tax: estate or inheritance tax guidance (UK IHT, US estate tax; India has none today but income on inherited assets is taxed).
11. Liabilities: should Trove also discover debts (loans, cards) so executors can settle them? (Recommended: yes, show them, never pay from estate funds.)
12. Data retention after settlement and what happens to a Plan vault after release.
13. Accessibility and assisted channels for elderly and low-literacy users (voice, regional languages, phone support).
14. Grief-aware design: tone, pacing, ability to pause, involve a trusted helper.
15. Offline evidence: posting physical letters (Plan Mode reminders) and scanning paper post (Discovery).
16. Metrics and research ethics for using real cases to improve models (consent, de-identification).

## 31. Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| AA (Account Aggregator) | India's consent-based framework for sharing financial data between regulated entities |
| AIS / Form 26AS | Indian tax statements listing income, interest, dividends and high-value transactions |
| CAS | Consolidated Account Statement of demat and mutual fund holdings |
| DEA Fund | RBI fund that receives deposits unclaimed for 10 years |
| EPFO / UAN | India's Employees' Provident Fund Organisation / Universal Account Number |
| IEPF | Investor Education and Protection Fund (unclaimed shares and dividends, India) |
| Lead | A possible asset built from one or more signals; not a fact until confirmed |
| Signal | One extracted observation from a source (for example "SMS from bank X, card ending 1234") |
| Nominee | Person named to receive an asset (by an institution or in Trove Plan Mode) |
| Legal heir | Person entitled under succession law; may differ from the nominee |
| Probate / letters of administration | Court documents that give authority to administer an estate |
| Release gate | A check that must pass before vault contents are released |
| k-of-n quorum | k of the n verified nominees must confirm a death claim |
| Cooling-off period | Waiting time between a valid claim and release, during which the owner can veto |
| Envelope encryption | Data encrypted with a data key, which is itself encrypted with a master key |
| Crypto-shredding | Deleting the key so encrypted data becomes unreadable |
| Shamir secret sharing | Splitting a secret into parts so that only a set number of parts can rebuild it |
| RUFADAA | US uniform law on fiduciary access to digital assets |
| UDGAM | RBI portal to search unclaimed deposits across banks |
